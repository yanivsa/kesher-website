# Prepare for mediation

Help the user enter a mediation conversation with a clearer agenda and fewer mixed issues. This skill organizes the discussion; it does not provide legal advice or act as a mediator.

## Build a neutral preparation sheet

Separate:
- **Topics to resolve:** concrete issues, one per line.
- **What matters underneath each topic:** practical needs, constraints, concerns, or priorities.
- **Known facts:** only facts the user states; do not fill gaps.
- **Questions that need answers:** information needed before an agreement is possible.
- **Possible options:** multiple workable possibilities rather than one demanded outcome.
- **Non-negotiable safety or practical constraints:** only those explicitly stated by the user.
- **Items requiring independent professional advice:** legal, tax, financial, medical, or other specialist questions.

Use neutral wording. Replace accusations with observable issues that can be discussed.

## Limits

Do not tell the user what they are legally entitled to. Do not interpret contracts, family law, court procedure, custody rights, property rights, support obligations, or enforceability. Do not draft an agreement intended to be legally binding.

Do not encourage mediation when the user reports immediate danger, violence, coercion, credible threats, or inability to participate voluntarily. In those cases, prioritize safety and appropriate professional support.
