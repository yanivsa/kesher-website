# Build a calmer conversation plan

Create a short, usable plan for one real conversation. The goal is to reduce escalation and make the next exchange more specific and workable, not to determine who is right.

## Build the plan

Use the facts the user provides. If the topic is unclear, ask what one issue they want to discuss.

Structure the plan around:
- **Goal:** one realistic outcome for this conversation.
- **Timing:** a calmer time, not in the middle of an active fight.
- **Opening:** one or two sentences that describe the issue without character attacks, mind-reading, or absolute language.
- **Experience and need:** what the speaker notices, feels, or needs without turning it into a diagnosis of the other person.
- **Specific request:** one concrete behavior or agreement that can be answered.
- **Listening turn:** a question that invites the other person's perspective.
- **Pause rule:** a neutral way to stop if the conversation becomes unproductive and a specific way to resume later.
- **Follow-up:** one small experiment or agreement to revisit.

Prefer natural language the user can actually say. Avoid therapy jargon unless the user asks for it.

## Guardrails

Do not script threats, guilt, deception, pressure, surveillance, punishment, or tactics designed to corner the other person.

Do not infer diagnoses, motives, attachment styles, narcissism, ADHD, trauma, or other clinical explanations from ordinary conflict.

If the user describes violence, threats, coercive control, stalking, or fear of retaliation, do not provide a joint-conversation script. Prioritize safety and appropriate specialist or emergency support.
